How the radar prices a launch that has not happened

The snapshot's tokenized protocols are the evidence, never the board. For each category the radar takes the median market-cap/TVL ratio of the protocols in it whose tokens already trade — each ratio winsorized to [0.002, 10] first, so one mispriced microcap cannot move a category — and shrinks it toward the global median with weight comps / (comps + 6), so a category with two comparables leans on the market as a whole while one with forty stands mostly on its own. The blended multiple is clamped to [0.02, 2.5]. A pre-token protocol is then priced from its own record alone: TVL times its category multiple, times a momentum factor from its 7-day TVL change (bounded, so a doubling week adds 35% and a halving week removes 17.5%), times a maturity factor that pays up to +25% for four years of operating history without a token, times a size factor that discounts the smallest protocols by up to 7.5%. No protocol name is special-cased: the same arithmetic scores every row, and each row's detail view shows its own numbers.

Rows below the watchlist's $50M floor must pass the same product tests the watchlist states — not a bridge, liquid-staking, restaking or exchange category, no version marker or wrapped / staked word in the name — because a wrapped or bridged product of a token that already trades is not a launch still to come. "Records no token" is the snapshot's statement, and the board repeats it in those words: a protocol may carry a token its record does not show.
